# Neat Nest Brand Book

## [BRAND_NAME] Brand name and architecture
Neat Nest is the master brand. "Nest" frames the home as a tended, living space that is built and maintained, and a nest is also woven, which links the name to the textile heritage of the packaging. "Neat" is the functional promise of order without sterility. FlexFold is the product family name, with four variants: FlexFold Lite, FlexFold, FlexFold Heritage Edition and FlexFold Pro. Accessories carry plain descriptive names (Liner Set, Charcoal Odour Insert, Wheel Kit). Retired names that must not appear in any material: NestEase, Neatnest, FlexiFold.

## [BRAND_TAGLINE] Tagline and supporting line
The tagline is "Tidy Starts Here." "Tidy" is the emotional outcome, "Starts" frames the product as the entry point to a more ordered home, and "Here." closes with confidence. The product line "Carry Less. Roll More." is used only on FlexFold packaging and product pages, never as the brand tagline. Rejected alternatives: "Built for the Load You Actually Carry", "Order, Every Day.", "Where Tidy Lives.", "Because Laundry Never Stops.", "Small Space. Big Order."

## [BRAND_PURPOSE] Purpose, vision and mission
Purpose: a task performed several times a week in every home has never been treated as worth solving properly, and the person who absorbs that invisible labour deserves a product that works and a presentation that treats their home with dignity. Vision: to redefine what a laundry basket is allowed to be, engineered like infrastructure and dressed like heritage, starting in India's urban apartments. Mission: engineer home-organisation products around the real physical movements of household labour (the hallway, the staircase, the humid utility corner, the third year of ownership) and present them through packaging that carries authentic Indian textile traditions into everyday life.

## [BRAND_VALUES] Brand values
Quiet Respect: acknowledge invisible labour without turning it into a slogan or guilt trip. Earned Durability: durability proven by disclosed tests and warranty, not claimed on a label. Woven Heritage: Indian textile craft treated as living design language with accurate motifs and regional attribution. Honest Simplicity: every feature maps to a named pain point. Everyday Dignity: daily routines deserve to be taken seriously. Grounded Optimism: specific, credible claims; no sweeping lifestyle promises. Considered Growth: expand only where the core promise deepens.

## [BRAND_VOICE] Tone of voice
Voice is warm, specific and plain. Speak to the person who does the work (persona: Anjali, the invisible laundry manager) with empathy for time scarcity and mental load. Validate, never moralise. Use concrete numbers and test results instead of adjectives. Do: "Rolls down two flights without lifting." Don't: "Revolutionary laundry experience." Never use guilt, gender stereotypes or "clean home" lifestyle clichés. Never claim a cause or benefit the product has not been tested for.

## [BRAND_CBBE] Brand equity plan (Keller CBBE)
Salience: be recalled at trigger moments, namely a basket breaking, moving into a new apartment, or a first "fix it properly" organisation purchase. Category frame is "integrated laundry storage", not "a basket". Performance: durability backed by warranty, mobility, hygiene, right-sized capacity. Imagery: sits beside premium home-organisation brands, not the cleaning aisle; imagery centres the person doing the labour. Judgements: quality, credibility, superiority on four dimensions. Feelings: competence, relief, being seen. Resonance: accessory repeat purchase, renter-to-renter referral, community content on the invisible labour of running a home, reviews requested after 4-6 weeks of real use.

## [BRAND_VISUAL] Visual identity, colour palette and photography
Core palette (from the craft heritage system): Indigo Night #2F415D (primary, text and logo), Terracotta Clay #C47A4F (accent, calls to action), Warm Ivory #F4EBDD (backgrounds), Desert Sand #D5B893 (secondary surfaces), Olive #7A7748 (support), Charcoal #2F2A26 (body text on light). Product photography: real apartments, natural light, the product in use in a hallway or on a staircase. Packaging artwork is flat textile pattern, no shadows. The logo is a lowercase "neat nest" wordmark with a woven-nest mark.

## [BRAND_PACKAGING] India Craft Heritage packaging system
Every FlexFold Heritage Edition ships in a premium cardboard sleeve whose structure is identical across variants: brand identity, one short story about the craft, key motifs, curated palette and standard product information. Only the artwork and story change. Five collections: Block Print (Rajasthan and Gujarat), Bandhani and Leheriya tie-dye (Rajasthan), Kalamkari (Andhra Pradesh and Telangana), Madhubani (Mithila, Bihar) and Ikat (Odisha and Telangana). Launch with two (Block Print and Ikat), expand to five in Phase 2. Lead sleeve by city: Mumbai Block Print, Pune and Bengaluru Ikat, Hyderabad Kalamkari, Delhi NCR Madhubani. Each sleeve credits the craft region. A royalty or artisan-collaboration fee per sleeve is planned for Phase 2.

## [BRAND_MESSAGING] Messaging hierarchy
Level 1 (why): solves space, movement, durability and hygiene together, unlike commodity baskets that solve none or design-led imports that solve one. Level 2 (proof): tilt-and-roll wheels, reinforced handle joint, three removable compartments, ventilated panels with antimicrobial liner, folds to 8 cm, three-year warranty. Level 3 (value): cost per year. At Rs 1,799 over a four-year life FlexFold costs about Rs 450 a year. A Rs 400 basket costs about Rs 270 a year if it lasts 18 months, but about Rs 800 for a heavy user who replaces it twice a year, so this message targets heavy users first, alongside the removal of the carrying, sorting and odour problems.
